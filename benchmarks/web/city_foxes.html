Why city foxes are thriving

Red foxes first moved into British cities in the 1930s, when new suburbs with large gardens spread across the countryside. Today they live in almost every town. Researchers think there are around 150,000 of them in urban areas alone.

City foxes eat a surprisingly varied diet. Scavenged food makes up about half of it, but they also catch pigeons, rats and insects. Some even learn which houses leave food out for them at night.

A different kind of fox

Scientists comparing skulls found that urban foxes have shorter snouts and smaller brains than their rural cousins. This pattern resembles the early stages of domestication seen in dogs. It does not mean that foxes are becoming pets, but it shows how quickly animals adapt to living near people.

Foxes are mostly active at dusk and during the night. In winter, when they look for a mate, their screams can wake whole streets.
